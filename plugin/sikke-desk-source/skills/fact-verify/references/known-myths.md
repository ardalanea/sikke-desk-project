# Known money myths and traps

Starting list; add to it whenever the fact-checker marks a card Myth.

| Claim | Status | What to say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Einstein called compound interest "the eighth wonder of the world" / "most powerful force in the universe" | No reliable evidence he said it | "A quote often credited to Einstein, with no evidence he said it" |
| "Salary" comes from Roman soldiers being paid in salt | Disputed; the salt link is a later folk explanation, payment in salt is not documented | "The word is linked to Latin *sal* (salt); the idea that soldiers were paid in salt is not proven" |
| Nathan Rothschild made a fortune by learning of Waterloo first | Legend, popularised by a hostile pamphlet; carries antisemitic baggage | Run only as a careful debunk, or skip |
| Tulip mania ruined the Dutch economy | Historians (e.g. Anne Goldgar) find the economic damage was limited and accounts exaggerated | "A real price bubble, but its damage was far smaller than the legend says" |
| Marie Antoinette / any royal quotes about money | Usually unsourced | Check before use |
| "Money is the root of all evil" | Misquote; the source text says the *love of* money | Quote accurately |
| Bitcoin was created by a named, confirmed person | Satoshi Nakamoto's identity is unconfirmed | Never name anyone as Satoshi |
| "The first ATM was in…" | Several competing "firsts" by definition | Name the machine and say what made it first |
| Ponzi invented the Ponzi scheme | The trick predates him (e.g. Sarah Howe, 1870s Boston; William Miller, 1899) | "Named after Ponzi, but not invented by him" |
