**Fact (40–120 words, plain, no hype):** On 29 September 2026 the SEC charged TSAI Pro Ltd. and TSAI Capital Foundation in a civil complaint, alleging they invited investors to "rent" AI trading bots for fees of $100 to $500,000, promoted through a website, WhatsApp and Facebook, with a recruitment-commission structure. The SEC says TSAI posted a falsified SEC regulatory certificate and a fabricated Form D filing to look officially registered. No bots and no real trading existed. About $2.8 million was raised from roughly 1,715 investors. This is a civil complaint, not a criminal charge — nothing proven in court, no individual named or convicted.

**The surprising detail:** The company didn't just claim to be SEC-regulated — it allegedly manufactured a fake government filing and posted a doctored "certificate" to make the forgery look official.

**Echo today:** Forging the appearance of regulatory approval is an old con-artist move (compare Alves dos Reis's forged contracts, or Thérèse Humbert's invented heirs) applied to a 2026 AI-crypto pitch — and delivered over WhatsApp, the exact kind of channel this magazine itself publishes to.

**Uncertain points:** The ~1,715 investor count and $2.8M figure are SEC allegations from the complaint, untested in court. No individual has been named or charged — write about the entities, not a person.
