**Fact (40–120 words, plain, no hype):** On 5 April 1933, President Franklin D. Roosevelt signed Executive Order 6102, requiring everyone in the continental United States to hand over gold coin, gold bullion and gold certificates to the Federal Reserve by 1 May 1933, in exchange for a fixed $20.67 per troy ounce. People could keep only up to $100 worth (about 5 ounces) plus coins of recognised collector value. Violators faced a fine of up to $10,000, up to 10 years in prison, or both. The order was issued under the WWI-era Trading with the Enemy Act, as amended by the 1933 Emergency Banking Act. It was repealed effective 31 December 1974.

**The surprising detail:** Less than a year later, the Gold Reserve Act of 1934 revalued gold to $35 an ounce — the government profiting from gold it had just compelled citizens to sell at the lower, fixed price.

**Echo today:** Modern capital controls (bans on foreign-currency withdrawal, crypto exchange freezes) follow the same basic logic: stop the public from holding value the state needs mobile.

**Uncertain points:** None significant. Worth noting in copy: a wartime law meant for seizing enemy assets was used against ordinary Americans' own savings.
