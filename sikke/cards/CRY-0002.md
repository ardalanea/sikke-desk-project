**Fact (40–120 words, plain, no hype):** On 24 September 2026, an attacker who had gained high-level access through a zero-day flaw in a third-party security product sent two tiny "probe" transfers — 0.184 ETH and 193 TRX — small enough to stay under Bitget's automated risk-alarm thresholds. Neither tripped an alert. The same access was then used to drain roughly $361 million across 17 transactions on eight blockchains over about 71 minutes; total losses reached $388 million from hot and warm wallets. Bitget's own systems flagged a discrepancy just seven minutes after the large transfers began — not fast enough to stop the theft. Cold wallets and customer balances were unaffected.

**The surprising detail:** The attacker essentially unit-tested the exchange's fraud detection with transfers too small to notice — before running the real heist.

**Echo today:** A concrete lesson for exchange security teams: a tiny, anomalous test transfer is often the quiet rehearsal before a real breach, not noise to ignore.

**Uncertain points:** Bitget suspects North Korea-linked hackers (citing overlaps with known Lazarus/TraderTraitor wallet infrastructure); this is the exchange's own and an analytics firm's attribution, not confirmed by any government or law-enforcement body. No individual has been named or charged. Write "Bitget says" / "suspects" — never state the attribution as fact.
