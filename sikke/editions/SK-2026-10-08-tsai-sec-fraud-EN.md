## Telegram (EN)

**They "rented" out an AI trading bot. The bot never existed — and neither did the government approval they showed to prove it.**

Starting in September 2024, TSAI Pro invited investors to rent an "AI trading bot" for $100 to $500,000, promoted over WhatsApp and Facebook. Bring in a friend, and you earned a commission.

When investors asked if it was legal, TSAI Pro allegedly had an answer: a certificate claiming registration with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Investigators say it was forged — along with a government filing posted beside it.

There was no bot. No trades ever happened. On 29 September 2026, the SEC filed a civil fraud case: about 1,715 investors had paid in roughly $2.8 million.

Nothing has been proven in court, and no individual has been charged. But the method is centuries old: forge the official stamp, and people stop asking questions.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A company convinced investors its AI trading bot was legitimate by forging the one thing that was supposed to prove it: the regulator's own approval. Starting in September 2024, TSAI Pro Ltd. invited investors to "rent" an AI trading bot, with fees from $100 to $500,000. It recruited through a website, WhatsApp and Facebook, paying commissions to anyone who brought in a new investor. When prospective investors asked whether the company was legitimate, it had an answer: a certificate showing registration with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, and a matching government filing. The SEC says both were fabricated. There was no bot. No trades were ever placed with investor money. On 29 September 2026, the SEC filed a civil fraud complaint. About 1,715 investors had paid in roughly $2.8 million. The case is civil, not criminal — nothing is proven in court, and no individual has been named.

What it teaches us:
Forging the appearance of approval is older than finance itself — only the paperwork changes.
A recruitment commission is a pyramid in disguise, whatever the product.
"Where did this come from?" is worth asking before "how much can it make me?"

Where else do you see organisations asking for trust before they've earned it?
