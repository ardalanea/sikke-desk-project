# Sikke Voice: the reader-load rule

Every post is a weight the reader picks up. If the exercise feels good, they keep lifting to the last line. If it is too heavy or boring, they drop it in the first two seconds. Write so the weight always feels lighter than it is.

## The four laws

1. **Grip (the first line).** The first line must create a question the reader needs answered. Lead with the surprising detail, a number that sounds wrong, or a tiny scene. Never open with background, a date alone, or "Did you know".
   - Weak: "In 1024, the Song dynasty introduced paper money."
   - Strong: "Europe was still paying in metal. China had already printed 600 years of paper money."
2. **Reps (every block earns the next).** Each sentence or slide answers the last question and opens a new one. If a sentence answers nothing and asks nothing, cut it.
3. **Rest (white space and rhythm).** Short paragraphs (1–3 lines on a phone). One idea per paragraph, one idea per slide. Alternate a long sentence with a short one. Numbers made physical: "a stack of notes as tall as a house" beats "4 trillion".
4. **The finish (payoff).** End with the twist, the lesson or the echo to today, and then one light prompt (a question or "save this for the next time someone says…"). Never end on a summary of what was already said.

## Weight classes (match tone to length)

| Weight | Where | Length | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feather | WhatsApp, IG reel on-screen text | 40–90 words | One fact, one twist, playful |
| Light | Telegram post, Facebook post, IG caption | 90–180 words | Curious, conversational, one mini-story |
| Medium | IG/FB carousel | 7–10 slides, ≤25 words per slide | Story with a build-up; each slide a cliffhanger |
| Heavy | LinkedIn, Telegram long thread | 180–300 words (LinkedIn), up to 450 (thread) | Smart and calm; a business lesson from history |

The heavier the post, the more often it needs a "rest": a line break, a one-line paragraph, a slide turn.

## Tone by subject

- **Oddities, firsts, records:** playful and wide-eyed.
- **Cons and heists:** detective story; tension, then how they were caught. Respect the victims; no glamorising the fraudster.
- **Crashes and money lock:** human and serious; ordinary people's experience; never mocking, never alarmist.
- **Crypto:** curious and clear, no hype, no jargon without a plain explanation.
- **Myths:** confident and friendly, never smug.

## Always

- Plain words. If a 15-year-old can't follow it, simplify.
- Real specifics: names, places, numbers, objects.
- One emoji at most per post and only where it is natural for the platform; never as bullet points.
- Active voice; present tense for storytelling scenes is welcome.

## Never

- "Did you know…?", "In today's world…", "Let's dive in", "game-changer", "mind-blowing" (show it, don't say it).
- Investment advice, price predictions, "buy" or "sell" language.
- Exaggerating the card. The post may be more exciting than the card, never more certain.
