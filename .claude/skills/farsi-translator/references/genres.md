## News and journalism

- Formal written register, neutral tone, short active sentences. No rumor,
  no exaggeration, no adjectives the source did not use.
- Lead with who did what. Persian news style puts the actor and time early:
  «بانک مرکزی ترکیه روز پنجشنبه نرخ بهره را ۲٫۵ واحد درصد کاهش داد.»
- Headlines: short, verb usually in the simple past or present, no final
  period, no article-style padding. Persian headlines often drop the verb
  «است» and keep an active verb: «دلار به بالاترین سطح سه‌ماهه رسید».
  English headline conventions (present tense for past events, "to" for
  future, commas for "and") must be decoded, not copied: "Fed to cut rates"
  → «فدرال رزرو نرخ بهره را کاهش می‌دهد».
- Attribution verbs: «گفت»، «اعلام کرد»، «افزود»، «تأکید کرد»، «هشدار داد»،
  «به گزارش …»، «به نقل از …»، «به گفتهٔ …». Keep the strength of the
  original verb: "claimed" → «مدعی شد», "said" → «گفت», "admitted" →
  «اذعان کرد» / «پذیرفت».
- Every figure in the source appears in the translation, attributed the way
  the source attributes it (forecast, estimate, official figure).
- Titles: «رئیس‌جمهور»، «نخست‌وزیر»، «وزیر خزانه‌داری»، «رئیس بانک مرکزی»،
  «سخنگو». Put the title before the name: «جروم پاول، رئیس فدرال رزرو» or
  «رئیس فدرال رزرو، جروم پاول».
- Citations with date and time use Persian digits and format, for example
  «رویترز — ۱۱ اکتبر ۲۰۲۶، ساعت ۱۴:۳۰».

Rial vs. toman: translate foreign currencies as they are. Mention toman or
rial only when the source does.

## Marketing, ads and slogans

- This is transcreation: keep the promise and the feeling, not the words.
- Short sentences, concrete benefits, warm tone. Persian ad copy is less
  superlative-heavy than American copy; "amazing", "revolutionary",
  "game-changing" translated literally («شگفت‌انگیز»، «انقلابی») sound like
  spam. Say what the product does.
- Decide «شما» (trust, premium, services, finance) or «تو» (youth, lifestyle,
  intimacy) once and keep it.
- Calls to action are short imperatives or nouns: «همین حالا ثبت‌نام کنید»،
  «خرید»، «بیشتر بدانید»، «رایگان امتحان کنید».
- Slogans: offer two or three options. Rhythm and sound matter more than
  literal accuracy; a touch of rhyme or parallelism is very Persian.
- Check that product names and wordplay have no unfortunate meaning or sound
  in Persian.

## App and website text (UI strings)

- Keep it short. Persian words are often longer than English ones and
  buttons are small. «ذخیره» beats «ذخیره کردن» on a button.
- Buttons and menu items: noun or verbal noun («ذخیره»، «حذف»، «ویرایش»،
  «ارسال»، «تنظیمات»، «ورود»، «خروج»، «بازگشت»، «لغو»، «تأیید»).
  Instructions and messages: polite imperative with «شما» endings
  («رمز عبور خود را وارد کنید»).
- Standard terms: Sign in «ورود», Sign up «ثبت‌نام», Log out «خروج»,
  Password «رمز عبور», Username «نام کاربری», Search «جستجو», Settings
  «تنظیمات», Profile «پروفایل / حساب کاربری», Notifications «اعلان‌ها»,
  Download «دانلود», Upload «آپلود / بارگذاری», Share «اشتراک‌گذاری»,
  Loading… «در حال بارگذاری…», Error «خطا», Retry «تلاش دوباره», Next
  «بعدی», Back «قبلی / بازگشت», Done «تمام», Submit «ثبت / ارسال», Cancel
  «لغو», Delete «حذف», Edit «ویرایش», Dashboard «داشبورد».
- Placeholders stay intact but move to where Persian grammar needs them:
  "{count} new messages" → «{count} پیام جدید»; "Welcome back, {name}!" →
  «{name} عزیز، خوش آمدید!». Remember no plural after numbers, so one string
  usually covers singular and plural.
- Error messages: say what happened and what to do, without blame:
  «اتصال برقرار نشد. دوباره تلاش کنید.»
- Do not end button labels or titles with a period. Use «…» for ongoing
  actions.
- The layout is right-to-left. If a string contains an arrow or says "left"
  or "right" about the interface, check whether it must be mirrored.

## Subtitles and dialogue

- Colloquial register (شکسته) for spoken dialogue, written the way Tehranis
  speak: «نمی‌دونم»، «می‌خوام برم»، «چی شده؟»، «بذار ببینم». A period drama,
  formal speech or documentary narration stays in written register.
- Short lines. Condense rather than translate every filler word; the viewer
  has about two seconds per line.
- Each character keeps a consistent voice and a consistent تو/شما toward
  each other character.
- Swearing and slang: match intensity with real Persian usage, toned to the
  platform. Do not sanitize unless asked, and do not invent vulgarity.
- Songs and on-screen text follow the user's instruction; by default
  translate on-screen text that matters to the plot.

## Business email and letters

- Opening: «با سلام و احترام،» (formal), «سلام آقای/خانم …، وقت بخیر» (semi-
  formal), «سلام … جان،» (friendly).
- Closing: «با احترام،» / «با سپاس،» / «ارادتمند،» followed by the name.
- Persian business writing softens requests: «ممنون می‌شوم اگر…»،
  «لطفاً در صورت امکان…»، «خواهشمند است…» (very formal). English directness
  ("Please send it by Friday") is fine in meaning but add ordinary courtesy:
  «لطفاً تا جمعه ارسال بفرمایید.»
- Honorific verbs for the reader in formal letters: «بفرمایید»، «ملاحظه
  فرمایید»، «تشریف بیاورید». For yourself use plain verbs.
- Keep it as brief as the original. Do not add ceremonial paragraphs that are
  not there.

## Legal and official documents

- Precision outranks elegance. Do not simplify, merge or split clauses in a
  way that could change scope. Keep every "shall", "may", "must" distinct:
  «مکلف است / باید»، «می‌تواند / مجاز است»، «نباید / حق ندارد».
- Use established legal vocabulary: «طرفین»، «متعهد»، «متعهدٌله»، «قرارداد»،
  «ماده»، «بند»، «تبصره»، «فسخ»، «خسارت»، «مسئولیت»، «حق‌الزحمه»،
  «قانون حاکم»، «مرجع حل اختلاف»، «فورس ماژور (قوهٔ قاهره)».
- Formal register is expected here, including some forms avoided elsewhere
  («می‌باشد»، «مذکور»، «فوق‌الذکر») where the genre uses them.
- Numbers: give amounts in digits and, where the source does, in words.
- Names, registration numbers, addresses and dates must be exact. For
  certified use, tell the user that an official sworn translator
  (مترجم رسمی) is required; this translation is a working draft.

## Technical and academic text

- Use the term the Persian-speaking field uses. First mention of a
  specialized term: Persian term, then the original in parentheses once:
  «یادگیری تقویتی (Reinforcement Learning)». After that, Persian only.
- Many technical communities use the English loan in practice («فریم‌ورک»،
  «دیباگ»، «سرور»، «کامیت»). For developer-facing text prefer what developers
  say; for textbooks and official documents prefer the Academy term if it is
  in circulation («نرم‌افزار»، «سخت‌افزار»، «پایگاه داده»، «شبکه»،
  «الگوریتم»).
- Code, commands, API names, file names, keys and error strings are never
  translated. Comments inside code are translated only if asked.
- Academic register: impersonal and measured («نتایج نشان می‌دهد»، «در این
  پژوهش»). Avoid the calqued "we" of English papers when it sounds odd;
  «در این مقاله … بررسی می‌شود» is the usual form.
- Units and formulas stay as they are, left-to-right.

## Literary text

- Voice first. Identify the narrator's rhythm, vocabulary level and humour,
  then find the Persian voice that does the same work.
- Dialogue may be colloquial while narration stays written; that split is
  normal in Persian fiction. Be consistent within each.
- Keep imagery; replace only what would be meaningless. Keep foreign names
  and settings foreign.
- Poetry and lyrics: tell the user whether you prioritised meaning, rhythm or
  rhyme, and offer an alternative for key lines.

## Chat and social media

- Colloquial, short, the way people actually type: «مرسی»، «دمت گرم»،
  «چه خبر؟»، «اوکی»، «فعلاً». Half-space rules still apply in careful
  posts; in very casual chat, match the user's own style.
- Hashtags: no spaces, words joined with underscore («#اقتصاد_ایران»).
  Mentions and handles stay in Latin as they are.
- Emoji stay where the source has them. Do not add new ones.
- Platform terms: Like «لایک», Follow «فالو / دنبال کردن», Share «اشتراک‌گذاری
  / شِیر», Story «استوری», Post «پست», Comment «کامنت / نظر», DM «دایرکت»,
  Channel «کانال», Group «گروه».
