# Source-language traps and foreign names

Read the section for the language you are translating from, plus "Writing
foreign names in Persian" whenever the text contains names.

## Any source language

- Translate directly from the source. Do not go through English in your
  head: that imports English structure on top of the original and
  mis-transliterates names.
- No character of the source script may remain in the Farsi (Cyrillic,
  Chinese, Hebrew, Greek, Devanagari and so on). Keep an original-script
  form only if the user explicitly asks for it.
- If part of the source is unreadable, garbled or cut off, translate what is
  there and say so in a note. Never fill a gap with a guess.

## English

Nearly every pattern in `translationese.md` comes from English. In addition:

- **Headlinese and telegraphic style** (dropped articles, present for past,
  "to" for future, noun stacks like "rate cut hopes") must be expanded into a
  real sentence before translating.
- **Noun stacks:** "central bank rate decision meeting" → work out the
  relations: «نشست بانک مرکزی برای تصمیم‌گیری دربارهٔ نرخ بهره».
- **Phrasal verbs** are single meanings: "turn down" «رد کردن», "put up
  with" «تحمل کردن», "come up with" «پیدا کردن / ارائه دادن».
- **Billion/trillion:** billion = «میلیارد», trillion = «تریلیون» (or
  «هزار میلیارد»), million = «میلیون». "Billion" is never «بیلیون».
- **"You" generic** ("you never know") → impersonal: «آدم نمی‌داند» /
  «معلوم نیست».
- **Understatement and irony** ("not bad", "quite good", "interesting…")
  need their real force rendered.
- **Dates:** watch US month/day order (03/04 = ۴ مارس). If the order is
  ambiguous and the context does not settle it, say so.

## Arabic

The shared script and vocabulary make Arabic the most deceptive source. Many
Arabic words exist in Persian with a different meaning.

| Arabic word | Means in Arabic | Persian word means | Translate as |
|---|---|---|---|
| انقلاب | coup | revolution | کودتا |
| ثورة | revolution | — | انقلاب |
| صورة | picture, image | face / form | عکس، تصویر |
| عكس | opposite, reverse | photo | برعکس، وارونه |
| شراب | any drink | wine | نوشیدنی |
| مجتمع | society | (residential) complex | جامعه |
| جامعة | university | society | دانشگاه |
| كثيف | dense, intense | dirty | متراکم، شدید |
| مزخرف | ornamented | nonsense | تزئین‌شده، آراسته |
| تعارف | getting acquainted | ritual courtesy | آشنایی |
| حوصلة | craw (of a bird) | patience, mood | چینه‌دان |
| رقيب | watcher, censor; sergeant | rival | ناظر / گروهبان |
| مسؤول | official (noun) | responsible / official | مقام، مسئول (by context) |
| إدارة | administration, management | office | دولت («إدارة بايدن» = دولت بایدن)، مدیریت |
| الخارجية | foreign ministry | — | وزارت خارجه |
| دوائر | circles (political) | — | محافل |
| ملف | file, dossier (issue) | — | پرونده |
| مباحثات | talks | — | گفت‌وگوها، مذاکرات |

Other points:

- **Word order:** Arabic is often verb–subject–object. Persian is
  subject–object–verb. Rebuild, do not shuffle.
- **Sentence chains with «و» and «فـ»:** Arabic strings clauses together at
  length. Split into separate Persian sentences.
- **Emphatic and formulaic openers** («إنّ»، «قد»، «أمّا … فـ») usually
  disappear; do not render them as «همانا» or «به تحقیق».
- **Dual and gendered forms** have no Persian counterpart; use «دو» or
  «هر دو» only where number matters.
- **Arabic plurals and tanwin** should not be copied wholesale: «تطورات» →
  «تحولات», «إجراءات» → «اقدامات» or «تدابیر».
- **Names:** drop «ال» where Persian usage does («السعودیة» → «عربستان
  سعودی», «القاهرة» → «قاهره», «الأردن» → «اردن», «الجزائر» → «الجزایر»,
  «المغرب» → «مراکش» for the country, «مصر» → «مصر»). Write Arabic names
  with Persian letters (ی، ک) and Persian spelling conventions.
- **Religious formulas** stay in their familiar form in religious texts;
  in news, follow Iranian press usage.
- **Dialects:** if the source is colloquial Arabic (Egyptian, Levantine,
  Gulf, Iraqi), translate into colloquial Persian for dialogue and say which
  dialect you assumed if it affected meaning.

## Turkish

- **Agglutination hides whole clauses in one word.** Unpack them into normal
  Persian clauses: «gelemeyeceğimi söyledim» → «گفتم نمی‌توانم بیایم».
- **Long pre-nominal participial phrases** («dün aldığım kitap») become
  Persian relative clauses after the noun: «کتابی که دیروز خریدم».
- **Reported/inferential «-miş»** carries "apparently / reportedly":
  «گویا»، «ظاهراً»، «گفته می‌شود». Do not lose it in news.
- **Shared vocabulary with shifted meaning:**

| Turkish | Meaning | Not |
|---|---|---|
| misafir | guest | مسافر (traveller) |
| hasta | sick, patient | خسته (tired) |
| dava | lawsuit, case; cause | دعوا (quarrel) |
| ziyaret | visit (any) | زیارت (pilgrimage) → دیدار، بازدید |
| tatil | holiday, vacation | تعطیل (closed) → تعطیلات |
| mektep/okul | school | مکتب (doctrine, old school) → مدرسه |
| hükümet | government (cabinet) | حکومت (regime) → دولت |
| devlet | the state | دولت (government) → حکومت، دولت by context |
| memur | civil servant | مأمور (agent, officer) → کارمند |
| muhalefet | the opposition | مخالفت → اپوزیسیون، مخالفان |
| hâlâ | still | حالا (now) → هنوز |
| kira | rent | کرایه (fare) → اجاره |
| pazar | Sunday; market | بازار only for market → یکشنبه |

- **Letters to Persian:** c = ج, ç = چ, ş = ش, j = ژ, ğ is silent or «غ»
  by established form («Erdoğan» → «اردوغان»), ö/ü ≈ «و» («Özgür» →
  «اوزگور»), ı ≈ «ی» or a short vowel («Yıldız» → «ییلدیز»).
- **Institutions and terms:** TCMB / Merkez Bankası → «بانک مرکزی ترکیه»;
  TL → «لیره»; TBMM → «مجلس ترکیه»; Cumhurbaşkanı → «رئیس‌جمهور»;
  KKTC → «جمهوری ترک قبرس شمالی» / «قبرس شمالی»; Lefkoşa → «نیکوزیا»
  (or «لفکوشا» for the northern part by context); Girne → «گیرنه»;
  Gazimağusa → «فاماگوستا»; TÜİK → «مرکز آمار ترکیه»; Borsa İstanbul →
  «بورس استانبول».
- Turkish number format uses "." for thousands and "," for decimals
  (1.250,50). Read it correctly before converting to Persian digits.

## French, German, Spanish, Italian, Russian

- **Formal/informal "you"** (tu/vous, du/Sie, tú/usted, ты/вы) maps directly
  onto «تو» / «شما». Preserve it; it is information the English lacks.
- **Grammatical gender** vanishes. If gender carried meaning, express it
  with a noun («خانم»، «آقا»، «دختر»، «پسر»).
- **German:** unpack compounds («Zinssenkungserwartungen» → «انتظار کاهش
  نرخ بهره») and split the long verb-final sentences. Konjunktiv I in news
  marks reported speech: use «به گفتهٔ …» or «گفت که…».
- **French:** many Persian loanwords are French, so the French form is often
  already the Persian one («تلویزیون»، «اتوبوس»، «ژانویه»). Beware of faux
  amis with those loans: «actuellement» is «در حال حاضر», not «در واقع».
- **Spanish/Italian:** dropped subjects map well onto Persian; keep them
  dropped. Diminutives and augmentatives need adjectives or tone, not
  literal suffixes.
- **Russian:** no articles and free word order, so definiteness must be
  inferred for «را» and «ـی». Aspect pairs map onto Persian simple past vs.
  imperfect («کرد» vs. «می‌کرد»). Patronymics are kept in formal reference
  and dropped where Persian press would drop them.
- **European number format:** "1.250,50" (German, Spanish, Italian, Turkish)
  and "1 250,50" (French, Russian) mean ۱٬۲۵۰٫۵۰. Read first, then convert.

## Chinese, Japanese, Korean

- No spaces, articles, plurals or (often) subjects in the source: infer them
  from context and write complete Persian sentences.
- Topic-prominent sentences ("As for X, …") should become ordinary Persian
  subject sentences, not «دربارهٔ X، …» every time.
- Honorific levels (Japanese keigo, Korean speech levels) map onto
  تو/شما plus honorific verbs («فرمودند»، «تشریف آوردند») where natural.
- Four-character idioms and proverbs: translate the meaning.
- Large numbers: 万 = ۱۰٬۰۰۰ and 亿 / 億 = ۱۰۰٬۰۰۰٬۰۰۰. "3亿" is «۳۰۰
  میلیون», not «۳ میلیارد». This is the most common serious error from these
  languages.
- Names: family name comes first in Chinese, Korean and (in native order)
  Japanese; keep the order Persian press uses («شی جین‌پینگ»، «کیم
  جونگ‌اون»، but «شینزو آبه»). Pinyin to Persian: q = چ, x = ش,
  zh = ج or ژ by established form, c = تس («چینگ‌دائو»، «شیان»،
  «ژجیانگ»، «گوانگ‌ژو»).
- Reign-year and lunar dates: keep as given and add the Gregorian date in
  parentheses if the source or context supplies it.

## Writing foreign names in Persian

1. **If an established Persian form exists, use it**, even when it differs
   from the pronunciation («اینشتین»، «شکسپیر»، «افلاطون»، «ارسطو»،
   «ناپلئون»، «مسیح»، «پکن»).
2. **Otherwise transliterate from the original language's pronunciation**,
   not from the English spelling: German «شولتس» (Scholz), «مرکل»; French
   «مکرون», «رنو» (Renault); Spanish «خورخه» (Jorge), «سانچز»; Italian
   «جورجا ملونی»; Dutch «روته».
3. **Write long vowels, skip most short ones**, as Persian spelling does.
   Use «و» for o/u and «ی» for i/e-long; initial vowels need «ا/آ/ای/او».
4. **Persian has no th, w, or initial consonant clusters in native words**:
   th → «ت» or «ث» by convention («اسمیت»), w → «و», and clusters are
   written as they are («استالین»، «اسپانیا»، «فرانک»).
5. **Use ZWNJ inside multi-part names where Persian press does**:
   «جین‌پینگ»، «وال‌استریت»، «نیویورک‌تایمز».
6. **Brands and products:** established Persian spellings where they exist
   («گوگل»، «اپل»، «مایکروسافت»، «تسلا»، «سامسونگ»، «آمازون»). Model names,
   tickers and codes stay in Latin («iPhone 17»، «S&P 500»، «BTC»).
7. **Media outlets:** «رویترز»، «بلومبرگ»، «آسوشیتدپرس»، «فرانس‌پرس»،
   «بی‌بی‌سی»، «سی‌ان‌ان»، «فایننشال تایمز»، «وال‌استریت ژورنال»،
   «نیویورک تایمز»، «گاردین»، «الجزیره»، «آناتولی».
8. **Be consistent.** One person, one spelling for the whole document.
9. When a name has no established form and its pronunciation is uncertain,
   give your best transliteration and add the Latin form in parentheses at
   first mention.
