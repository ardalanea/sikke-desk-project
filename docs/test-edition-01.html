Tabriz, 1294: the paper money the bazaar refused

Approved · Ardalan · 1 Oct 2026 Fact-checked · Verified Standards · Cleared EN · FA · TR 5 platforms

In 1294 the Mongol ruler of Iran ordered Tabriz to use Chinese-style paper money, and refusing it was punishable by death. The bazaar stopped trading and, about two months later, the paper was withdrawn. Chosen as the lead because it is surprising, local to our Iranian and Turkish readers, and carries a lesson about trust in money.

Before you read the posts

What the checks caught

Fact-checker corrections

  • Duration. A press source said the paper money lasted three days. Two scholarly sources say about two months. Posts say "about two months".
  • Europe comparison. The reporter wrote "350 years before Europe's first central-bank notes". Stockholm's 1661 bank was not a central bank, and the gap is 367 years. Now "more than 360 years before Europe's first banknotes".
  • Death penalty. It was the penalty in the order. No source records anyone executed for refusing, so posts never say people were killed.
  • Cut detail. "Printed on mulberry bark" came from one weak source and was removed.

Standards editor fixes

  • Extra claim removed. The English draft said "gold and silver coins". The card only says coins. Fixed in all three languages.
  • Reel hook toned down. "Could get you killed" became "was punishable by death".
  • Religious wording. The declaration of faith on the notes is now stated plainly, not used as a selling point.
  • Turkish neutrality. A line framing the Ilkhanate through Turkish school history was removed.

Needs your judgment

  • Farsi Telegram uses a pun on our name: «چاو هیچ‌وقت سکه نشد» ("the čāv never caught on", using سکه). Keep it?
  • Turkish uses "kelime-i şehadet" for the declaration of faith. Right tone for your readers?
  • English Telegram is about 1,030 characters, just over the limit for a photo caption. Post it as a text message, or we cut a line.
  • Slide 3 image: use a free Met Museum image of a Chinese note. British Museum images may not allow commercial use.
